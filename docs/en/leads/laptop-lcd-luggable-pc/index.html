Decide before you build

  • Current stage The author pairs a 17-inch LCD from a GPU-failed laptop with a model-specific controller and retired desktop parts to complete a luggable Mark I computer.
  • Practical readiness A matching LCD can become a standard-video display and part of a custom all-in-one computer.
  • User value Includes real experience matching a controller to a panel and integrating a complete system.
  • Follow-up recommendation Continue if the exact panel number is known and size is acceptable; bench-test before building an enclosure.

Device fit and limits

Known fit or worth checking

  • The author's Dell Inspiron E1705 panel or LCDs with matched controllers
  • Still-functional retired ATX/desktop components

Not established or unsuitable

  • Buying controllers by screen size alone
  • Users seeking a thin, low-power portable computer

Gaps, next step, and risk

  • Why it remains E0 The controller must match the exact panel number; the author later moved to a new-screen Mark II and stopped developing the salvaged version.
  • Next review step Record panel number, controller firmware, backlight type, native resolution and power specifications.
  • Risk and stop condition CCFL backlight boards generate high voltage; enclose bare panels, sharp metal and power electronics.
