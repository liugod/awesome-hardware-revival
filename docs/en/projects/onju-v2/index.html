Practical decision notes

  • What was holding it back A second-generation Nest Mini still has a useful enclosure, microphones, speaker and touch controls, but its stock motherboard does not directly provide a custom conversation pipeline.
  • What it can do afterwards With an Onju ESP32-S3 board installed, the device can become a network voice endpoint while ASR, the LLM and TTS run on a LAN host or optional cloud services.
  • Assessment This is a board replacement, not a stock-firmware jailbreak. Before starting, confirm a second-generation Nest Mini, the Onju v2 board documentation, first-flash access and an always-on host. Google Assistant, Cast and stock multi-room behavior do not remain after the swap. The shared-I2S route also cannot listen while playing audio. KitAgain has not reproduced the build hands-on.
  • A good fit if People who have confirmed a second-generation Nest Mini, can disassemble and perform the first flash safely, will maintain an always-on voice stack, and accept losing stock functions.
  • Skip this route if People who cannot confirm the generation, must retain Cast or stock multi-room behavior, require simultaneous listening and playback, or expect a software-only download with no board swap or server maintenance.

Checks after the build

  • Record the device generation before power-off; photograph disassembly and keep the original board, cable routing and fasteners intact.
  • Before reassembly, verify first flash, microphones, speaker, touch controls, mute switch, Wi-Fi and server separately.
  • Test behavior during server or network loss; if the route does not fit and original parts are undamaged, power off and reinstall the original board.
