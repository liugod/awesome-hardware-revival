DECIDE BEFORE YOU BUILD

Current stage

This is a multi-user pattern rather than one project. Browser dashboards and kiosk control are demonstrated; long-term safety still needs device-specific checks.

Practical readiness

A reasonably recent spare tablet or phone can often serve as a Home Assistant dashboard terminal for display and light control, but that does not establish safe concealed continuous power.

Device fit and limits

Known fit or worth checking

  • Community guidance suggests roughly 2 GB RAM or more, recent Android/iOS and a usable WebView/browser
  • The reported cheap 11-inch Android tablet displays a dashboard but is slow and has randomized-MAC issues
  • Xiaomi tablets, a Samsung OLED tablet and iPad 9 are candidates for further trial

Not established or unsuitable

  • Very old WebView or low-memory devices rendering complex dashboards smoothly
  • Aged tablets without charge limits, with heat or worn batteries in permanent wall use
  • An ESP32 display running a full Lovelace web dashboard directly

Why this lead is useful

Tests performance, kiosk behavior, power-loss recovery and charging before committing to wiring and a wall mount.

Should you continue?

Run it on a desk first; mount only after performance and charge limits pass. Retire swelling, unusually hot or deformed devices immediately.

Why it remains E0

This is a distributed pattern rather than one package. Browser engines, Android versions, charging control, wall cooling and long-term battery safety vary widely.

Next review step

Collect browser version, charge limits, reboot recovery and 30-day operation for exact tablet models before producing a formal guide.

Risk and stop condition

A community author reports a battery swelling under long-term charging. Old batteries, concealed wiring and continuous power require separate review.
