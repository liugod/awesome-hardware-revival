Turn a rotary phone into a color-selection lamp

The author adds control electronics and lighting to an old GTE rotary phone, using dial pulses for colors and the hook switch to select odd/even positions.

L21 · E0 · Sources reviewed 2026-10-21 · No hands-on hardware tests

DECIDE BEFORE YOU BUILD

Current stage

The author adds control electronics and lighting to an old GTE rotary phone, using dial pulses for colors and the hook switch to select odd/even positions.

Practical readiness

Reuses the mechanical dial and enclosure as an interactive lighting object.

Device fit and limits

Known fit or worth checking

  • Pulse-dial phones with intact mechanics
  • Low-voltage interactive pieces retaining the original appearance

Not established or unsuitable

  • Continued ordinary telephone service
  • Devices not fully isolated from outside lines

Why this lead is useful

A compact example of preserving tactile mechanics while giving the object a new interaction.

Should you continue?

Continue if dial contacts can be identified and isolated; prefer nondestructive work on collectible complete phones.

Why it remains E0

The page is an author showcase with limited circuit files and no broad interface guide for other rotary phones.

Next review step

Identify the controller, dial-pulse input, power design and complete wiring files.

Risk and stop condition

Disconnect completely from telephone lines and ringing voltage; never attach the modified unit to an active public network.
