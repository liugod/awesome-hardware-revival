Decide before you build

  • Current stage The author designs a CM4 replacement board for a 2010 iPad to reuse the enclosure, battery, touch, LCD, antennas, speaker and buttons, with a reported booting system.
  • Practical readiness Advanced builders may reuse much of the original structure for Linux/Android, while no mature consumer kit exists.
  • User value Unusually aims to retain many original components instead of using only the enclosure.
  • Follow-up recommendation Continue if board review, battery work and prototype cost are acceptable; others should wait for stable hardware.

Device fit and limits

Known fit or worth checking

  • The author's 2010 first-generation iPad scope
  • Developers capable of fine-pitch soldering and PCB debug

Not established or unsuitable

  • Applying directly to other iPad generations
  • Users seeking a software-only route to modern apps

Gaps, next step, and risk

  • Why it remains E0 Custom PCB, display and battery management remain complex; a complete BOM, long-term battery safety and all peripherals are not packaged for ordinary users.
  • Next review step Review the latest PCB revision, manufacturing files, LCD/touch drivers, charge protection and system image.
  • Risk and stop condition The aged lithium battery and custom power board need separate validation; a booting CM4 does not prove safe charging.
