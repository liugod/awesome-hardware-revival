Adapter board for salvaged Kindle displays

A Hackaday project documents high-voltage drive rails, an adapter PCB and ESP experiments for 39-pin e-paper panels, including a successful black-and-white output log.

L06 · E0 · Sources reviewed 2026-10-21 · No hands-on hardware tests

DECIDE BEFORE YOU BUILD

Current stage

Revision 2 generated the intended rails and black-and-white output on a specific backup panel; grayscale and broad panel support are unfinished.

Practical readiness

Useful to developers who can identify a 39-pin panel, solder SMD parts and handle positive and negative high-voltage rails; not a drop-in Kindle route.

Device fit and limits

Known fit or worth checking

  • The tested 39-pin salvaged panel and panels with the same verified electrical specification
  • ESP8266 or STM32 experiments after rechecking timing, RAM and PCB revision
  • Developers needing Gerbers, PCB files and driver source

Not established or unsuitable

  • Assuming all six-inch Kindle or all 39-pin panels are compatible
  • Ordering without checking diode orientation, FPC soldering and revision
  • Complete grayscale, a finished enclosure or long-term stability

Why this lead is useful

The power rails, connector, PCB/code and failure logs reduce reverse-engineering work, but this is not plug-and-play.

Should you continue?

Continue only if you can read the panel number, measure high voltage and review the PCB; choose a finished route for an ordinary second-screen need.

Why it remains E0

The project has been quiet for years, connectors and waveforms vary by panel, and the author recorded a screen damaged during an earlier board attempt.

Next review step

Verify exact panel numbers, PCB revision, BOM, code license and the availability of manufacturable files.

Risk and stop condition

The driver uses multiple positive and negative high-voltage rails. Incorrect wiring can destroy a panel; this is not a universal Kindle guide.
