Decide before you build

  • Current stage The tutorial presents a complete author build reusing the Play:1 enclosure and drivers; KitAgain has not reproduced it or reviewed every revision.
  • Practical readiness A failed or unused Play:1 can become a Raspberry Pi powered speaker after removing the original board. It requires disassembly, soldering, amplifier matching and mains safety.
  • User value A relatively complete build that exposes the Pi, amplifier, power and software path; its main value is mechanical and electrical guidance.
  • Follow-up recommendation Continue with soldering and mains-safety experience when the drivers work; do not attempt it for software-only unlocking or without safe discharge handling.

Device fit and limits

Known fit or worth checking

  • A Sonos Play:1 enclosure with working 4-ohm speaker assembly
  • Raspberry Pi 3 Model A+ as used, with Raspberry Pi Zero 2 explicitly offered as an alternative
  • A DAC/amplifier for 4-ohm speakers and an isolated 20 V supply

Not established or unsuitable

  • Assuming other Sonos models share the same space, impedance and wiring
  • Retaining original mainboard functions, Sonos multi-room protocol or certification
  • Users without mains-equipment safety experience handling board capacitors

Gaps, next step, and risk

  • Why it remains E0 KitAgain has not checked Sonos hardware revisions, power/amplifier matching, full cost or tutorial licensing, and has not reproduced the build.
  • Next review step For one exact Play:1 revision, document disassembly, driver impedance, isolated power, audio software and acceptance checks.
  • Risk and stop condition The original unit uses mains power and board capacitors may retain dangerous voltage. This is not a low-risk first disassembly project.
