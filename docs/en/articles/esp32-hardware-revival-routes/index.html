ESP32 / ESP8266 hardware revival: start with the device, then choose the firmware

An ESP32 is a means, not the goal. It can give old hardware networking, displays, audio or protocol bridging, but only after the device and chip are identified. Start there to avoid the wrong board or a damaged device.

Identify the hardware before searching for firmware

Record the complete model, board photos, radio-module markings, supply voltage, visible serial pads and whether the original device still boots. Back up readable firmware and configuration, then find a specific recovery route. A brand name is not a chipset: the same Tuya enclosure may ship with ESP8266, BK7231, RTL87xx or another module across revisions.

Mains-powered devices can expose a dangerous path between a USB serial adapter, computer ground and the open power board. Without isolation, power-off verification and suitable experience, do not open a smart plug, wall switch or lamp. Begin with a USB-powered board, low-voltage LED strip or external sensor.

Smart plugs, switches and bulbs: replace cloud control with local control

For a confirmed ESP8266/ESP8285 or supported ESP32, examine Tasmota and its web configuration, MQTT/HTTP control and recovery documentation. For BK72xx, RTL87xx or LN882x modules, compare OpenBeken with ESPHome's LibreTiny platform and match the CPU, board, flash layout and pins exactly.

A successful flash does not prove that relays, buttons, status LEDs and power metering work correctly. Find a pin map for the same module and board revision. Test power loss, reboot, offline control and recovery on one device before touching the rest of a batch.

Electronic shelf labels: turn low-power e-paper into a calendar or status card

OpenEPaperLink combines access-point firmware, tag firmware and flashing interfaces. For explicitly documented tag types, it can drive calendars, room status, sensor readings or simple images. A setup commonly needs an ESP32 access point, a suitable radio tag and a matching flashing jig.

Similar enclosures can contain different controllers, display drivers and radio protocols. If the exact tag type, PCB marking, radio chip or programming pads cannot be established, document the teardown and stop rather than applying firmware from a nearby model.

LED strips and controllers: build ambient, status or notification lighting

WLED is a strong route for pairing an ESP32 with addressable pixels such as WS2812B. Reusing an existing controller depends on a flashable MCU, the strip protocol, its power supply and the output circuit. Analog RGB strips need drivers such as MOSFETs; their load must not be connected directly to GPIO.

Confirm rated voltage, maximum current, data direction and common ground. Long wires or 5 V pixels may require level shifting, and power wiring should be sized and fused for the load. Begin with a few pixels at low brightness.

Old stereos: add network playback without asking the ESP32 to power speakers

Squeezelite-ESP32 can make a suitable ESP32 board a Lyrion Music Server player, with Bluetooth, AirPlay and Spotify Connect as additional modes. A common route is an ESP32 plus I2S DAC feeding line-level audio into the stereo's AUX input, while the original stereo remains the power amplifier.

The project documents a typical minimum of 4 MB flash and 4 MB PSRAM and says WROOM modules normally lack the required PSRAM. Confirm the board, DAC and stereo input. Never connect a speaker output to the ESP, and do not expect a bare development board to drive passive speakers.

BLE, sub-GHz radio and infrared: turn old devices into signal inputs

ESPHome Bluetooth Proxy can extend Home Assistant coverage for BLE devices. ESP32 supports advertisement forwarding and active GATT connections, but the target still needs a compatible Home Assistant integration. A proxy does not automatically decode an unsupported proprietary protocol.

OpenMQTTGateway can translate BLE, infrared and several radio-module signals into MQTT. Actual capability depends on the ESP board, transceiver, frequency, modulation and decoder. Rolling-code remotes, encrypted protocols and region-restricted transmission cannot be treated as ordinary fixed-code devices.

Minimum acceptance: complete one real job before copying the build

Define one observable outcome: a plug works without the cloud; a tag updates after reboot; an LED strip survives full load without overheating; a stereo plays continuously and recovers after power loss; or a proxy reliably receives one real sensor. Record board, chip, firmware, wiring, power and both success and failure conditions.

This guide summarizes public project documentation. KitAgain has not certified these hardware combinations. Pause whenever the model, chip, power path or recovery route is unclear, then share redacted board photos and markings in the community for further identification.
