Upcycle a failed Sonos Play:1 with Raspberry Pi

An official Raspberry Pi tutorial removes the original mainboard, keeps the enclosure and drivers, and adds a Pi, DAC and amplifier with follow-up software routes.

L09 · E0 · Sources reviewed 2026-10-21 · No hands-on hardware tests

DECIDE BEFORE YOU BUILD

Current stage

The tutorial presents a complete author build reusing the Play:1 enclosure and drivers; KitAgain has not reproduced it or reviewed every revision.

Practical readiness

A failed or unused Play:1 can become a Raspberry Pi powered speaker after removing the original board. It requires disassembly, soldering, amplifier matching and mains safety.

Device fit and limits

Known fit or worth checking

  • A Sonos Play:1 enclosure with working 4-ohm speaker assembly
  • Raspberry Pi 3 Model A+ as used, with Raspberry Pi Zero 2 explicitly offered as an alternative
  • A DAC/amplifier for 4-ohm speakers and an isolated 20 V supply

Not established or unsuitable

  • Assuming other Sonos models share the same space, impedance and wiring
  • Retaining original mainboard functions, Sonos multi-room protocol or certification
  • Users without mains-equipment safety experience handling board capacitors

Why this lead is useful

A relatively complete build that exposes the Pi, amplifier, power and software path; its main value is mechanical and electrical guidance.

Should you continue?

Continue with soldering and mains-safety experience when the drivers work; do not attempt it for software-only unlocking or without safe discharge handling.

Why it remains E0

KitAgain has not checked Sonos hardware revisions, power/amplifier matching, full cost or tutorial licensing, and has not reproduced the build.

Next review step

For one exact Play:1 revision, document disassembly, driver impedance, isolated power, audio software and acceptance checks.

Risk and stop condition

The original unit uses mains power and board capacitors may retain dangerous voltage. This is not a low-risk first disassembly project.
