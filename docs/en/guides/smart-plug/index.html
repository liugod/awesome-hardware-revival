Local smart plugs: verify the chip, board, and safety conditions

OpenBeken, ESPHome, and Tasmota all require an exact hardware match. A product name may hide a changed chip, and mains devices need safe isolation and a recovery route.

Identify the internal hardware first

  • Match the complete model, regional electrical specification, PCB, and controller. The enclosure and product-page name do not establish identical internals.
  • OpenBeken targets documented BK7231-family and other listed chips, while ESPHome and Tasmota require a matching device configuration or template.

Choose from the exact-model page

  • Sonoff S31 and Shelly Plug S have exact-model source entries, but the current hardware revision and flashing method still need confirmation.
  • The Athom and Kogan entries remain model-level leads. Do not treat them as flash-ready firmware until chip, pins, flashing, and recovery have been read.
  • If energy monitoring matters, verify voltage, current, power, and accumulated values separately. A working relay does not prove metering accuracy.

Mains safety and acceptance

  • Open the enclosure only after disconnecting and isolating power. Never probe exposed energized hardware, and do not treat this guide as qualified electrical judgment.
  • Back up original flash and calibration data, then test switching, power-loss recovery, network failure, and local control separately.
  • KitAgain adds no safety certification, load test, metering calibration, or long-term hardware result.
