Decide before you build

  • Current stage The author combines an older laptop LCD, host and high-power LED into a projector/media center designed around a swappable laptop core, with a first prototype documented.
  • Practical readiness Suitable for optics and cooling experiments, not for users expecting a finished projector experience.
  • User value Shows a structure that reuses a whole laptop and allows later host replacement.
  • Follow-up recommendation Continue to learn projection optics; compare repair or a used projector for regular viewing.

Device fit and limits

Known fit or worth checking

  • Older laptops with working LCD and motherboard
  • Builders with optical, woodworking and thermal-design skills

Not established or unsuitable

  • Cracked or badly damaged panels
  • Quiet, bright and dependable home cinema

Gaps, next step, and risk

  • Why it remains E0 The project has been quiet for about a decade; brightness, optics, cooling, power and high-power LED safety lack current acceptance data.
  • Next review step Verify panel dimensions, Fresnel focal length, LED driver, measured temperatures and projected brightness.
  • Risk and stop condition High-power LEDs, intense light, heat and possible CCFL high voltage require shielding, thermal control and insulation.
