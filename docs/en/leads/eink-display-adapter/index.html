Decide before you build

  • Current stage Revision 2 generated the intended rails and black-and-white output on a specific backup panel; grayscale and broad panel support are unfinished.
  • Practical readiness Useful to developers who can identify a 39-pin panel, solder SMD parts and handle positive and negative high-voltage rails; not a drop-in Kindle route.
  • User value The power rails, connector, PCB/code and failure logs reduce reverse-engineering work, but this is not plug-and-play.
  • Follow-up recommendation Continue only if you can read the panel number, measure high voltage and review the PCB; choose a finished route for an ordinary second-screen need.

Device fit and limits

Known fit or worth checking

  • The tested 39-pin salvaged panel and panels with the same verified electrical specification
  • ESP8266 or STM32 experiments after rechecking timing, RAM and PCB revision
  • Developers needing Gerbers, PCB files and driver source

Not established or unsuitable

  • Assuming all six-inch Kindle or all 39-pin panels are compatible
  • Ordering without checking diode orientation, FPC soldering and revision
  • Complete grayscale, a finished enclosure or long-term stability

Gaps, next step, and risk

  • Why it remains E0 The project has been quiet for years, connectors and waveforms vary by panel, and the author recorded a screen damaged during an earlier board attempt.
  • Next review step Verify exact panel numbers, PCB revision, BOM, code license and the availability of manufacturable files.
  • Risk and stop condition The driver uses multiple positive and negative high-voltage rails. Incorrect wiring can destroy a panel; this is not a universal Kindle guide.
