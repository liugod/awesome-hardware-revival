DECIDE BEFORE YOU BUILD

Current stage

The author reuses a failed Thomson TG585 router case, switch, LEDs and power connector to house a home-automation prototype.

Practical readiness

Provides a low-voltage enclosure with indicators, button and easy access.

Device fit and limits

Known fit or worth checking

  • Failed low-voltage routers with intact cases
  • Arduino, sensor or doorbell-interface prototypes

Not established or unsuitable

  • Retaining original router networking function
  • Connecting an unknown original power board directly to the new project

Why this lead is useful

A low-complexity way to reuse common enclosures and interfaces from electronic waste.

Should you continue?

Worth doing after low-voltage and insulation checks; repair the whole router first if it remains viable.

Why it remains E0

This reuses the mechanics and simple parts rather than reviving the router board; PCB isolation and mounting vary by enclosure.

Next review step

Verify connector polarity, LED current limiting, board retention, openings and long-term power/battery design.

Risk and stop condition

Fully remove or isolate unknown original power circuits and prevent loose boards from shorting.
